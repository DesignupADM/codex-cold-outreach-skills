# Opening Lines

Use this reference when drafting the first line of a cold email or testing opener variants.

## What strong openers do

- Create immediate relevance
- Earn the next sentence
- Support the message angle instead of distracting from it
- Work in mobile preview text

## Best-performing opener families

### Signal-based observation

Use when you have a real company or role trigger.

Pattern:

```text
Noticed [specific signal] at [company].
```

Examples:

- `Noticed the recent expansion into LATAM.`
- `Saw the new pricing page rollout last week.`

### Problem-first hook

Use when the buyer pain is obvious and likely active.

Pattern:

```text
Many [role plural] hit [specific pain] when [context].
```

Examples:

- `Many RevOps teams hit lead-routing delays after rapid SDR hiring.`
- `Many affiliate teams lose partner activation speed during market expansion.`

### Referral or proof opener

Use only when truthful and specific.

Pattern:

```text
[Name/company/proof point] made me think this might be relevant.
```

Examples:

- `[Mutual contact] suggested I reach out.`
- `Work we did with a similar sportsbook made this worth sending.`

## Opener rules

- Keep it short
- Use one real signal, not three stacked facts
- Tie the opener to the offer within the next sentence
- Skip generic greetings and pleasantries

## Avoid

- `Hope you're well`
- `Just wanted to reach out`
- Empty congratulations
- Personalization that does not connect to the offer
- First lines that could be sent to any company in the market

## Testing guidance

If the user wants opener testing:

1. Write 3 to 5 distinct opener types
2. Keep the rest of the email as stable as possible
3. Judge winners by positive reply rate first

Do not test tiny wording changes before testing clearly different angles.
