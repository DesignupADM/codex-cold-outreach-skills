# Sequence Patterns

Use this reference when building a full outbound campaign rather than a single email.

## Sequence principles

- Each touch should introduce a new reason to respond
- Repetition is acceptable only when the message gets sharper
- Later emails should usually get shorter
- Proof and specificity should increase as the sequence progresses

## Choose sequence length from incremental value

Invest the strongest relevance and proof in the first two touches. Use the three- and five-touch patterns below as starting templates, not required lengths. Add later steps only when they introduce a credible new angle and produce useful incremental replies without worsening complaints or opt-outs. Do not impose a universal two-email cutoff or a fixed 60-day restart rule from an aggregate report.

For an initial draft, test roughly 50–125 words organized as context, value, and one ask; shorten or expand when the buyer's task requires it. This is a writing constraint to test, not a guaranteed performance optimum.

## Three-touch template

### Email 1: Relevance plus offer

- Open with the strongest angle
- State the offer clearly
- Use one light proof point
- End with a simple CTA

### Email 2: Proof or use case

- Bring a case study, benchmark, or sharper example
- Show why the offer is not generic
- Keep the CTA easier, not harder

### Email 3: New angle or polite close

- Use a different frame such as risk, upside, or timing
- Optionally use a short breakup note if the campaign tone allows it

## Standard 5-touch sequence

### Email 1

Primary relevance angle and core offer

### Email 2

Proof, benchmark, or mini case study

### Email 3

Alternative angle, objection handling, or role-specific insight

### Email 4

Short bump with a sharper CTA or a send-first asset offer

### Email 5

Respectful close or breakup email

## Good variation ideas

- Switch from pain to upside
- Switch from observation to proof
- Switch from meeting ask to permission ask
- Switch from company-level angle to role-level angle

## Bad sequence habits

- Re-sending the same email with tiny edits
- Increasing pressure without increasing relevance
- Adding more product detail instead of more buyer relevance
- Ending every email with the same CTA language

## Breakup email guidance

Breakup emails should be brief and respectful. They work best when they lower tension instead of adding manufactured urgency.
