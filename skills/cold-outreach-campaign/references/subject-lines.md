# Subject Lines

Use this reference when generating or improving subject lines for cold outreach emails.

## Core rules

- Optimize for relevance first, curiosity second
- Keep subject lines short unless extra context clearly helps
- Make them look like something a real person would send
- Avoid spam flags, fake urgency, and over-formatting
- Write several styles, then rank them instead of picking the first decent option

## Default target

Aim for:

- 1 to 4 words most of the time
- 5 to 7 words when specificity adds value
- One core idea per line

## Reliable styles

### Straight relevance

- `pricing model`
- `pipeline quality`
- `casino CRM`
- `LATAM growth`

Use when the audience is sophisticated and context matters more than cleverness.

### Curiosity with context

- `quick thought on retention`
- `idea for sportsbook onboarding`
- `noticed the affiliate push`

Use when you have a real angle and want a softer open.

### Problem or opportunity framing

- `drop-off after signup`
- `more qualified demos`
- `faster partner activation`

Use when the outcome is obvious and valuable.

### Proof-led

- `12% lift example`
- `what worked for [peer]`
- `playbook from [segment]`

Use when proof is specific and credible.

### Light pattern break

- `worth a look?`
- `left field idea`
- `not a mass note`

Use sparingly. Pattern-break subject lines work best when the body copy is equally human and concise.

## Selection rubric

Choose finalists that score well on:

1. Relevance to the buyer and offer
2. Clarity on first read
3. Natural tone
4. Distinctness from the other options
5. Consistency with the email body

## Avoid

- All caps
- Excess punctuation
- "Re:" unless it is true
- Clickbait
- Empty curiosity with no anchor
- Overused promo language such as `free`, `guaranteed`, or `act now`
- Long lines crammed with keywords

## Practical output pattern

Default to this structure:

1. Ten to fifteen options
2. Top three to five ranked
3. One-line note on why the top choice fits the campaign

If the user wants testing help, propose two to three distinct angles rather than tiny wording changes.
