# Copy Frameworks

Use this reference when drafting or refining cold email copy, subject lines, and calls to action.

## Core copy rules

- **Strict word count**: Aim for **50 to 85 words**. Never exceed 90 words on a cold first-touch.
- **F-pattern mobile scannability**: Limit paragraphs to 1 or 2 sentences. An email must be comfortably read on a smartphone screen in under 10 seconds.
- **Zero pleasantries**: Never begin with "Hope you're having a great week" or "My name is X and I work at Y." The first sentence must earn the second.
- **One email, one angle, one ask**: Competing propositions confuse the buyer; multiple links or CTAs reduce conversion.
- **WIIFM (What's In It For Me)**: Focus entirely on the prospect's workflow, bottleneck, or metric—not on your product's feature list.

## The Mobile Preview Text Pair

Over 60% of cold outbound is triaged on mobile devices. The **Subject Line** and the **first 35–40 characters of the body** (the preview snippet) function as a unified headline:

- **Subject Line**: 2 to 4 words. Keep it lowercase or sentence case. Neutral, peer-to-peer tone (e.g., `quick question re: churn`, `onboarding bottleneck?`, `hiring ramp idea`).
- **Preview Snippet (First Line)**: Immediately introduces the context or observation. Never waste this space with greetings, company names, or self-introductions.

### Examples of High-Performing Preview Pairs

| Subject Line | First 35–40 Characters of Body | Why It Works |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `churn question` | `Noticed you recently expanded into...` | Signals relevant research before the prospect even opens. |
| `onboarding bottleneck?` | `Most Head of People we speak with find...` | Names a shared peer friction immediately. |
| `22% ramp improvement` | `Saw your team is hiring 10 SDRs this quarter...` | Ties a real hiring signal to a credible metric. |

## Multi-Angle Copy Blueprints

Always draft at least two distinct angles so the campaign can be tested effectively.

### Angle 1: Problem-Led (Cost of Inaction)
Best when the buyer is already feeling the bottleneck and looking for relief.

```text
[Observation highlighting a common workflow bottleneck or inefficiency.]

[Why this usually costs time, revenue, or pipeline for leaders in their role.]

[How your product eliminates that friction + 1 concrete proof point or metric.]

[Low-friction interest CTA.]
```

*Example (65 words)*:
> Saw your engineering team is scaling past 40 developers this quarter.
> 
> Most VPs of Engineering at this stage find code review bottlenecks quietly delaying sprint releases by 3–4 days.
> 
> We built a GitHub-native review assistant that helped Stripe reduce PR cycle times by 35% without adding headcount.
> 
> Worth exploring if this could speed up your current sprint cycle?

---

### Angle 2: Insight / Trigger-Led (Perspective Shift)
Best when there is a recent company motion (funding, launch, hiring, leadership change) or a non-obvious perspective to share.

```text
[Signal observation: recent event, initiative, or public change.]

[A counter-intuitive insight or trend affecting their industry.]

[How your approach solves this new reality.]

[Permission-based CTA.]
```

*Example (68 words)*:
> Noticed you just rolled out self-service signup for your enterprise tier.
> 
> When SaaS companies introduce PLG motions, product telemetry often overwhelms sales teams, making it hard to spot genuine enterprise expansion signals.
> 
> We created an automated intent-filter that surfaces high-value accounts right inside Slack.
> 
> Mind if I send over a 2-minute video breakdown of how other PLG teams set this up?

---

### Angle 3: Proof-Led (Peer Credibility)
Best when credibility and verifiable ROI are the main differentiators.

```text
[Direct reference to an adjacent peer company or common industry challenge.]

[Specific, measurable outcome achieved (numbers, percentages, time saved).]

[How the same mechanism applies to the prospect's team.]

[Low-friction interest CTA.]
```

*Example (58 words)*:
> We recently helped Ramp cut invoice processing time from 3 days down to 40 minutes, eliminating 90% of manual data re-entry.
> 
> Given your current finance team headcount, I imagined manual reconciliation might be eating up similar bandwidth each close.
> 
> Open to seeing how they automated that workflow?

---

## The Interest-Based CTA Matrix

Match the ask to the friction level of the offer:

| Level | CTA Style | Example Phrase | Best Used When |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Level 1: Interest Gauge** *(Lowest Friction)* | Asks if the topic is relevant right now. | *"Worth a look?"*<br>*"Open to exploring this?"* | First-touch cold email; unverified intent. |
| **Level 2: Permission Ask** *(Curiosity-Driven)* | Asks permission to share a short asset. | *"Mind if I send a 2-min breakdown?"*<br>*"Should I email the 1-page summary?"* | Educational / insight-led angles. |
| **Level 3: Asset Offer** *(Self-Service)* | Offers a tailored diagnostic or audit. | *"Happy to send over the audit we ran on your pricing page if helpful."* | Demonstrating immediate upfront value. |
| **Level 4: Direct Meeting** *(Highest Friction)* | Asks for a scheduled calendar conversation. | *"Open to a 15-minute chat next Tuesday?"* | Warm referral or hyper-targeted Tier 1 executive outreach with verified intent. |

## Tightening Weak Copy: Fast Filter

Before delivering drafts, execute this checklist:
- [ ] Cut any sentence starting with *"I am reaching out because..."*
- [ ] Remove corporate filler: *"synergy"*, *"cutting-edge"*, *"seamless"*, *"streamline"*, *"game-changer"*.
- [ ] If a paragraph has more than 2 sentences, split or cut it.
- [ ] Verify total word count is between 50 and 85 words.
- [ ] Confirm there is exactly ONE question mark in the entire email (the final CTA).
