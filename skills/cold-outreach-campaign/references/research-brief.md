# Research Brief

Use this reference when the user has not provided enough detail to write strong outreach, or when personalization needs to be tied to a sharper commercial angle.

## Minimum brief

Collect or infer these six inputs:

1. Audience: company type, size, region, maturity, and buyer role
2. Goal: reply, intro call, pilot, referral, or reactivation
3. Offer: what is being proposed and what the prospect gets
4. Pain or upside: why the offer matters now
5. Proof: results, customers, benchmarks, expertise, or a credible observation
6. Constraints: tone, region, compliance limits, or banned claims

If the user only gives a product and target audience, assume a reply-focused campaign and note the assumption.

## Research hierarchy

Prioritize inputs in this order:

1. Role-specific priorities
2. Company trigger or context
3. Offer-to-problem fit
4. Credibility and proof
5. Tone calibration

The strongest personalization is not "I saw your website." It is a specific observation that connects to the prospect's likely priorities and the sender's offer.

## Good personalization sources

- Funding, expansion, hiring, launches, partnerships, geography changes
- Public product motion, pricing motion, or channel changes
- Obvious role-level responsibilities such as pipeline, retention, onboarding, risk, or margin
- Relevant customer proof from adjacent companies or similar business models

## Weak personalization signals

- Generic compliments
- Facts that do not connect to the offer
- Boilerplate "noticed you are growing"
- Personalization that is more interesting to the sender than the prospect

## Angle selection guide

Use one dominant angle per email:

- Cost of inaction: when the pain is clear and expensive
- Untapped upside: when the gain is easier to sell than the problem
- Trigger event: when timing is the strongest lever
- Proof transfer: when credibility is the main unlock
- Insight or point of view: when the sender has a differentiated observation

## Offer framing

Strong offers are:

- Easy to understand
- Narrow enough to feel real
- Low commitment for the first yes
- Tied to an outcome, not a vague capability list

Weak offers usually sound like "we help companies with growth" or "would love to connect and share more."

## Assumption handling

When information is missing:

- State assumptions briefly
- Use placeholders only where a human must insert specifics
- Keep the copy usable even before those specifics are added
- Avoid pretending uncertain facts are known
