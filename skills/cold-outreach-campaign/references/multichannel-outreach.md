# Multichannel Outreach

Use this reference when the user wants a cross-channel sequence that combines email, calls, LinkedIn, and other touchpoints.

## Why multichannel matters

One-off cold email is rarely enough. Buyers are distributed across different channels, and modern outbound works best when the outreach feels like a real relationship-building process rather than isolated touches.

The strongest sequences combine multiple channels because:

- buyers see different signals in different places
- each channel reinforces the message differently
- multiple touchpoints improve response quality and context
- buyer attention is fragmented and not all leads are equally active on email

## Core principle

The purpose of multichannel outreach is not to overwhelm the prospect. It is to create familiarity, relevance, and a credible path to conversation.

Each touch should add something new: a new angle, a stronger proof point, or a more relevant context.

## Typical channel roles

### Email

Use for direct value, clear relevance, and a clean request for a reply or next step.

### LinkedIn

Use for visibility, warm introductions, and relationship context. Good for comments, connection requests, or short personalized messages.

### Calls

Use when the message is clear enough to justify a quick conversation or when a buyer is already warmed by email or social context.

### Video or voice notes

Use when a human message will help cut through low-trust automation. Short, direct, and highly relevant videos can create a strong personal impression.

## Best practices for layering channels

- Start with a clear message and direct relevance
- Do not send purely automated sequences without human context
- Use LinkedIn to warm up other touchpoints rather than replace them
- Use calls and email to reinforce the same core value, not to rehash the same pitch
- Make the sequence feel coherent and commercially honest

## Good multichannel sequence logic

A simple sequence might look like this:

1. Tiered prospect list and relevance-based segmentation
2. Personalized email with a clear offer or problem angle
3. Short LinkedIn engagement or connection request with a relevant note
4. Follow-up email with a sharper proof point or different angle
5. Call or voice note when the buyer is warm enough to pick up
6. Final breakup or closeout touch with a respectful, low-pressure ask

## What works less well

- Auto-email-only campaigns with no human or external context
- Overuse of generic follow-up language
- A call with no prior context or relevance
- Repeatedly sending the same message across channels without evolving it

## Relationship-driving principle

If a prospect sees your brand, hears from you via a relevant email, and later sees a thoughtful LinkedIn touch, the combination creates a much stronger impression than any single message alone.

The goal is to make the buyer feel like they are dealing with a real professional who understands their problem, not a generic outreach machine.

## Practical output standard

When the user asks for a multichannel campaign, include:

- the primary channel sequence
- the specific purpose of each touch
- the message angle for each step
- how the sequence avoids repetition
- the likely next step or conversion trigger
- any required context for calls, LinkedIn, or email handoff
