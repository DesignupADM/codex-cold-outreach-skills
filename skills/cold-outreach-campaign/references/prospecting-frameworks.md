# Prospecting and Lead Prioritization

Use this reference when the user needs to choose who to target, how to rank prospects, or how to build a healthier outbound pipeline.

## Core principle

Outbound success is not about contacting every possible lead. It is about reaching the right buyers with a relevant message at the right moment.

The best teams triage their list by fit, urgency, and buying signal strength before they write a single message.

## Recommended lead tiers

### Tier 1: Highest-priority prospects

These are the best-fit accounts and people most likely to buy soon.

Include:

- Decision makers or champions in the best-fit ICP
- Accounts with active buying motion or timing pain
- Strong firmographic and role alignment
- Relevant intent, trigger, or recent company change

Use the most manual effort here:

- Deep research
- Personalized messaging
- Role-specific proof and offer framing
- Multi-channel follow-up with careful timing

### Tier 2: Strong but less urgent prospects

These prospects may be a fit but are not in a current buying window or need more qualification.

Use:

- Segmented messaging by role, industry, or trigger
- Light personalization tied to a real signal
- Shorter, more templated outreach with enough relevance to feel human

### Tier 3: Broad or lower-priority list

These are still worth touching, but not with heavy manual effort.

Use:

- Basic personalization only
- Clear, low-friction value language
- Broad but clean segmentation
- More automation and less custom writing

## Buying signals worth tracking

Look for signals such as:

- New funding or recent financing
- Hiring surges in relevant teams
- Product launches or expansion plans
- Website changes or new positioning
- Intent or activity around keywords, competitors, or category terms
- Role changes or team transitions
- Account moves that imply budget or process change

These signals are more valuable than generic list data alone.

## Research workflow

Before outreach, gather three layers of context:

1. The human
   - role and seniority
   - mutual connections or shared context
   - interests, recent posts, or professional signals

2. The company
   - product and ICP
   - stage, market, and growth motion
   - recent news, wins, or pain points

3. The buying context
   - key team or process problems
   - current priorities or likely constraints
   - how your offer supports that context

## Avoid common prospecting mistakes

- Treating every lead as equally important
- Using low-signal or stale data without verification
- Building lists without role, fit, or trigger logic
- Reaching out before the buyer has enough context to care
- Sending a generic pitch to a high-value account

## Good prospecting outputs

A strong prospecting brief should clearly answer:

- Who is the target buyer?
- Why are they a fit for this offer?
- What signal makes them relevant now?
- What pain, opportunity, or trigger matters?
- What value can be shown in one sentence?
- What is the low-friction next step?

## Practical rule

A good outbound engine does not chase volume alone. It builds a list that is specific enough to personalize and strong enough to justify the time spent.
