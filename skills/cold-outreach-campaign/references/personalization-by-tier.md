# Personalization by Tier

Use this reference when deciding how much research, writing effort, and specific tailoring to apply to each prospect.

## The rule

Personalization is mandatory, but the required level changes by lead value and buyer signal strength.

The goal is not to write a custom email for everyone. The goal is to spend your manual effort where it matters most.

## Tier 1: Hyper-personalization

Best for:

- Top-fit accounts
- Executive, high-seniority, or high-value prospects
- Buyers with clear current urgency or budget alignment

This is where the prospect should feel that you have done real homework.

Research should connect across:

- The person
- The company
- The buying environment or industry context

Strong examples include:

- A relevant post, interest, or personal detail
- A specific company signal like a new initiative or expansion
- An industry-specific pain that matches your offer
- A credible reference to what is likely important to their role

Recommended tactics:

- Use a personalized subject line or first line
- Tie the message to a real trigger or role-specific issue
- Keep the body concise but specific
- Demonstrate understanding without sounding overly familiar

A good Tier 1 email feels like a sharp, relevant note rather than a mass email with a few data points swapped in.

## Tier 2: Semi-personalized templates

Best for:

- Strong-fit prospects who are open to buying but not urgent
- Accounts with one or two relevant signals but limited time for full custom work

Use segmented templates built around real group characteristics:

- industry
- team function
- trigger type
- company stage
- recent change or operational need

Customize:

- subject line language
- first sentence or opening observation
- role-specific pain or outcome
- a small proof point or relevant benchmark

The template should still sound like it was written for a specific segment, not for everyone.

## Tier 3: Basic personalization at scale

Best for:

- Lower-priority or broad lists
- High-volume prospecting where manual research is not cost-effective

Use:

- first name and company name
- a clear, honest reason for reaching out
- relevant industry or role context
- a short, simple CTA

Basic personalization is still worthwhile, but it should not be mistaken for deep research.

## What makes personalization credible

Good personalization feels like this:

- It reflects a real business signal
- It names a likely problem or opportunity
- It aligns with the prospect's role and context
- It helps the buyer quickly decide whether this deserves attention

Bad personalization feels like this:

- generic compliments
- fake familiarity
- irrelevant personal detail with no business relevance
- forced references that do not support the message

## A practical framework

Use this split:

- Tier 1: 70% of your personalization effort
- Tier 2: 20%
- Tier 3: remaining effort

This protects time while still preserving quality where the opportunity is strongest.

## Common rule of thumb

A short, specific email that feels relevant is usually better than a longer email that feels generic.
