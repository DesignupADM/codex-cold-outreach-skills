# Social Selling and LinkedIn Engagement

Use this reference when building a social selling layer into outbound or when using LinkedIn as a trust-building channel rather than pure prospecting.

## The mindset

Social selling is not about spamming DMs or force-pitching people who engage with you. It is about helping people publicly, building trust, and becoming recognizable as a credible operator in a relevant space.

The core idea is simple: people buy from people they trust, and trust grows through consistent value, visible proof, and relevant interaction.

## The three principles of posting

### 1. Demonstrate credibility and authenticity

Your posts should reflect:

- real lessons from your work
- customer or market observations
- relevant wins and failures
- practical opinions grounded in actual experience

This makes your profile feel human and credible rather than self-promotional.

### 2. Tell stories of customer success and measurable outcomes

Share tangible proof. Examples include:

- increased SQLs
- reduced costs
- improved pipeline efficiency
- better retention or conversion
- clear operational gains

A blurry story or vague brag does not create trust. Specific numbers and context do.

### 3. Play the long game

Do not pitch in the first five minutes of a conversation. That often creates resistance.

Instead:

- publish useful content consistently
- engage thoughtfully
- build visibility over time
- let credibility and timing do their work

When people see you as a knowledgeable voice in a topic, they are more likely to find you when the problem becomes real.

## Posting framework

A strong social-selling content engine often uses:

- 3 to 5 recurring topics
- at least 2 topics tied directly to buyer pain or value
- 3 audience personas aligned to the target market

This creates an infinite matrix of relevant content topics without forcing random posting.

Good content themes include:

- lessons from sales execution
- objections and buying patterns
- customer case studies
- market observations
- tactical frameworks that help buyers make progress

## Who to engage with

Think in three groups:

### Prospects

The people who fit the buying persona. This is the main purpose of LinkedIn, but they are not the only group that matters.

### Close personal network

These are the people you know or have worked with. They are often the most important group to engage with because they can amplify your visibility through their network.

### Broader audience

People you do not know directly but who interact with your posts and comments. These relationships matter even if they do not convert immediately.

## How to comment well

The best LinkedIn comments are:

- thoughtful
- specific
- relevant to the post
- short enough to read quickly
- credible without being performative

Strong comments do more than say “great post.” They show that you understand the point, the problem, or the buyer context.

## The right response to questions

If a prospect asks a good question on your post, avoid being vague. Go a little further than expected.

Provide something useful, concrete, and relevant. This builds trust faster than a hard sell.

## LinkedIn DM and outbound rules

Use LinkedIn as a trust amplifier, not just a lead source.

Good practice:

- send personalized connection requests with a short note
- focus on relevant context rather than product deck dumping
- use voice notes or short videos when they feel human and specific
- move conversations into email only when the relationship is warm enough to continue naturally

Avoid:

- pitch-first DMs
- generic connection requests with no message
- aggressive follow-ups before any trust has been built

## Social selling is a relationship engine

The real value of LinkedIn is not immediate revenue. It is visibility, relevance, trust, and warm access.

When used well, social selling helps create:

- inbound conversations
- warmer outbound conversations
- more credible email or call outreach
- referrals and better stakeholder access

## Practical output standard

When asked to create a social selling plan, include:

- target personas and network groups
- content topics and angle ideas
- comment engagement strategy
- DM and warm follow-up plan
- how the LinkedIn activity connects to outbound email or calls
